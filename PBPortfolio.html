prashamsa.codesA data-driven look at crime trends across every WA county from 2016–2024, how rates shifted around COVID-19, which counties carry the most and least crime relative to their population, and how that gap has moved over time.

why this study

I wanted to see what a real civic dataset could actually tell a story about, not just run numbers, but turn them into something a person could look at and understand. Crime data felt like a natural fit because it's public, it's messy in the ways real data usually is, and it directly shapes decisions that affect people's everyday safety. Understanding county-level crime patterns helps identify where crime is concentrated and how it shifts over time, which can support better policy decisions and prevention strategies, things that matters to WA residents, policymakers, law enforcement agencies, and local governments.

key findings

Spokane County has the highest crime rate relative to its population, while Ferry County has the lowest. Assault and property crimes make up the largest share of offenses statewide, and a reporting standard change from SRS to NIBRS in 2021 may affect how recent trends read. Dataset: 1,505 county-year observations across 299 variables, including total crime counts, violent and property crime counts, population totals, and crime rate per 1,000 residents. #DataScience#RStudio#WashingtonState#CrimeTrends#PublicSafety
Data from the Washington State Statistical Analysis Center — sac.ofm.wa.gov/data
